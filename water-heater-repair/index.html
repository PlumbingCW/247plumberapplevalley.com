Water heater trouble in Apple Valley, handled at any hour

When a water heater fails, it rarely does it at a convenient time. Tanks give out on cold January mornings and in the middle of laundry day, and a small drip can turn into a flooded garage before you notice. Our emergency water heater repair line in Apple Valley, CA is open 24/7, so call (760) 986-1999 at any hour.

If you see water under the tank, switch off the power at the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot or off, then close the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater.

We repair gas, electric and hybrid heat pump tank heaters, and we can look at tankless units as well. Repair means finding the part that failed, such as a heating element, thermostat, thermocouple, igniter or relief valve, and replacing it for a price you agree to first.

Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow. If the tank itself is leaking, repair is usually not possible, and we will tell you that early so you can decide on water heater replacement without paying for a repair that will not hold.

When a water heater problem cannot wait

Some heater problems are a nuisance and some are not. These should get a call right away, at any hour.

  • Water leaking from the tank body or base. Shut off power or gas and close the cold inlet. A leaking tank cannot be patched in most cases.
  • Water pouring from the relief valve discharge pipe. That valve is a safety device. Do not cap it, and call us.
  • A gas smell near the heater. Leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility first. We repair the heater after the area is declared safe.
  • Scalding water at the tap. A failed thermostat can overheat the tank. Turn the heater off and keep children away from hot taps.
  • Rumbling or popping from the tank. That usually means sediment is baked onto the bottom. It can wait a day, but do not ignore it.

No hot water: what we check first

No hot water is the second most common reason people call about a heater, and the cause depends on how the unit is powered.

Electric tank

Tripped breaker, a failed upper or lower element, a bad thermostat or a high-limit switch that needs a reset. We test each with a meter.

Gas tank

A pilot that will not stay lit, a worn thermocouple, a dirty burner or a failed gas control valve. Gas work is done with safety checks at each step.

Hybrid heat pump

Air filter, condensate drain, fan or compressor issues, and settings that switch the unit between heat pump and electric modes.

Tankless

Error codes, scale on the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet screen. See our tankless water heater page for flushing and descaling.

A leaking heater: where the water is coming from

Not every wet spot at the heater is a dead tank. Loose fittings on the hot and cold lines, a weeping drain valve and a relief valve that is passing water can all leak while the tank itself is sound. Those are repairs.

A leak from the tank body, or from the base with dry fittings above it, usually means the steel liner has corroded through. Water has reached the insulation and the outer jacket, and there is no durable fix. We confirm the source with a dry paper towel test, a flashlight and a moisture reading before we suggest anything.

If the leak is somewhere else in the house and you are unsure, leak detection can separate a heater problem from a pipe problem.

Apple Valley water and your water heater

Liberty Utilities serves about 81 percent of the Town from deep wells in the Alto sub-unit of the Mojave groundwater basin. Its 2022 report lists hardness between 57 and 130 ppm, with an average near 80 ppm, or about 4.7 grains per gallon. On the USGS scale, that falls in the moderately hard band.

For a water heater, moderate hardness means minerals slowly settle as sediment in the bottom of the tank and coat heating elements and burners. A yearly flush and a check of the anode rod, the sacrificial metal rod that corrodes first, can add useful years to a tank. If your address is served by another water purveyor, check your bill and the utility's latest water quality report before assuming these numbers apply.

According to the maker's guidelines, the relief valve is a safety device that must never be capped or bypassed. The California Plumbing Code treats it as a required part of a water heater.

When repair stops making sense

A heater that is old, leaking from the tank or failing for the second or third time in a short span is usually better replaced than repaired. The date on the data plate tells you when it was built, and a plumber can read the serial number if the label is hard to decode.

If you are on the fence, ask for both prices side by side. The comparison often settles the question, and we will give you one on the phone before sending anyone out.

What affects water heater repair cost

  • The failed part. An igniter, thermocouple or relief valve is a different parts bill than a gas control valve or heating element set.
  • Fuel type and heater style. Gas, electric and hybrid units use different parts and test equipment.
  • Access. A heater in a tight closet or a garage corner takes longer to service than one with open space around it.
  • Heater age. Older tanks may have corroded fittings that complicate even a simple repair.
  • Timing. After-hours repairs are staffed differently from a scheduled morning visit, so ask how timing changes your quote.

Insurance note: homeowner policies often cover sudden water damage from a heater failure but not the cost of the heater itself or wear over time. Take photos before cleanup, then call (760) 986-1999 for your real number.

Safety and code notes for water heater work

Every tank heater should have a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that runs to a safe spot. That valve is the last line of defense if the thermostat fails, so it must never be capped or plugged. Gas heaters also need correct venting and combustion air, and a carbon monoxide detector nearby is sensible.

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, and some water heater work needs a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division before replacing a unit or changing its fuel type. We will tell you what to expect when you call.
