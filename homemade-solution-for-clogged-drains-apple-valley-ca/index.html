Homemade Solutions for Clogged Drains in Apple Valley, CA

Most clogs start small, and a few household tools clear many of them. This guide ranks the do-it-yourself methods by how well they work, how safe they are and when to stop and call.

By the Apple Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 20264 min read

Start with the cause, then pick the tool

The right homemade fix depends on what is blocking the drain. Hair in a shower, grease in a kitchen sink and paper in a toilet are three different problems, and the same tool does not suit all of them. A minute of thought saves an hour of failed attempts.

A useful rule is to start with the gentlest method and move up. Mechanical tools, such as a plunger or a small hand auger, tend to work better than liquids, because a clog is a physical object that needs to be pushed or pulled out. Liquid recipes work best on light buildup and smells.

This guide focuses on tools and household recipes, and on the point where it is time to stop and call.

A sensible order to try homemade fixes

1
Hot tap water and dish soap. For a greasy kitchen sink, pour a squirt of dish soap into the drain, then run hot tap water for a minute or two. The soap helps break up fat.
2
Plunge properly. Cover the overflow opening with a wet cloth for a tight seal, fill the basin with a few inches of water and plunge steadily without lifting the cup.
3
Remove the trap. Place a bucket under the sink, loosen the slip nuts, and clear out what is inside the trap, then reassemble with the washers seated.
4
Use a hand auger. A small drain snake can pull hair or reach beyond the trap. Feed it slowly, turn the handle and pull the clog back out.

Homemade drain remedies ranked from useful to risky

  • Plunger. Safe and effective on most sinks and tubs when used with a tight seal.
  • Hand auger or plastic drain strip. Very good on hair, safe on most pipes if used gently.
  • Trap cleaning. Opens up the most common clog point under a sink.
  • Baking soda and hot water. A mild help on light buildup, not a fix for a plug.
  • Wet-dry vacuum. Can pull a shallow clog from a sink, though it takes care and a good seal.
  • Chemical openers. Risky for pipes, skin and septic systems, and not a good first choice.

What to avoid, and why

Boiling water is a common recommendation and a bad habit with plastic. It can soften PVC joints and crack porcelain toilets and sinks, so use hot tap water instead. Chemical drain openers can burn skin and eyes, damage some pipes and, if they fail, leave dangerous liquid in the pipe for a plumber to handle.

Never mix cleaners. Bleach and vinegar together make chlorine gas, and bleach with ammonia-based products makes chloramine gas. If you have used a chemical opener, tell the plumber before they open the drain, since the water in the pipe may be caustic.

A plunger and a snake have no such risk, which is why they come first. Our drain cleaning page explains what a plumber adds when they do not work.

Clogs in Apple Valley homes: septic, hard water and what to watch

About 70 percent of Apple Valley development is on septic according to Town planning documents, and the rest is on the Town sewer. That matters for homemade fixes. Harsh chemical openers and bleach can harm the bacteria that a septic tank depends on, so a plunger and an auger are the safer choices for a septic home.

Hard water also plays a part. Liberty Utilities reported 2022 hardness near 80 ppm on average, and scale roughens pipe walls so hair and soap catch more easily. Soaking strainers and aerators in vinegar helps, and a clean trap every so often prevents the slow buildup that ends in a full stop.

Signs a homemade fix is not enough

If you plunge and snake and the drain clears for a day, the clog is probably deeper, or the pipe has a belly or roots. If several drains run slow, or a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, the problem is in a shared line. If sewage comes up into a tub or floor drain, stop using water and call at once.

Those are not do-it-yourself jobs. A main line problem needs a cleanout, a long cable or a jetter, and a camera look afterward. Our sewer line cleaning page explains what happens. Homes in Victorville and Hesperia get the same help from the same line.

Habits that prevent most clogs

Use a strainer in every shower and kitchen sink and empty it into the trash. Wipe pans and plates before rinsing so fat does not enter the drain. Keep wipes, paper towels and feminine products out of the toilet, even if the package says flushable.

Run hot tap water after washing dishes, and once a month pour hot tap water down each drain. Learn where your cleanout is and where the main shutoff sits. A small habit now spares a middle-of-the-night call later.
