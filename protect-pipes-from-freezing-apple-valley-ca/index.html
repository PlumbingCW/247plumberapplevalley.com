How to Protect Pipes From Freezing in Apple Valley, CA

The desert is warm by day, but winter nights in Apple Valley fall into the low 20s. Pipes in garages, attics and outside walls are the first to freeze, and a little prep keeps them safe.

By the Apple Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 20264 min read

Why desert homes freeze

Many people think of freezing as a northern problem. In Apple Valley, the Town reports winter nights in the low 20s, an average January low near 31F and a record low of -1F. Homes here are often built with exposed pipes, uninsulated garages and plumbing on outside walls, because freezes are rare enough that builders did not plan for them.

A freeze damages pipes in a simple way. Water expands as it turns to ice, and the pressure can split a pipe or a fitting. The split often goes unseen until the ice melts, and then water flows. That is why a house can flood after the cold ends.

If a pipe has already split and water is flowing, close the main valve and call. Our burst pipe repair page explains what we do.

Pipes most at risk

  • Outdoor spigots and hose bibs, especially with hoses still attached.
  • Pipes in an unheated garage, including water heater lines and washer connections.
  • Attic and crawlspace pipes, where insulation is thin or missing.
  • Plumbing on outside walls, such as kitchen sinks on an exterior wall.
  • Irrigation lines and backflow devices above ground.
  • Tankless water heaters and their pipes in cold garages.

A simple freeze prep checklist

1
Disconnect hoses. Remove hoses from spigots and cover the spigots with insulated covers.
2
Insulate exposed pipes. Use foam pipe sleeves on pipes in garages, attics and crawlspaces.
3
Seal cold air. Close foundation vents, seal gaps where pipes enter the house and keep garage doors shut.
4
Know the shutoff. Find and test the main valve before the cold arrives.

On the coldest nights

On a night forecast for the low 20s or colder, open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so warm air reaches the pipes. Let a faucet drip slowly on the coldest lines, since moving water is harder to freeze. Keep the house heated, even if you are away.

If you travel in winter, leave the heat on at a moderate setting and ask someone to check the house. Shut off the main valve if you will be gone a long time and cannot keep the heat on. A frozen line in an empty home can flood it for days.

Freeze risk in Apple Valley, and what to do about the water supply

Apple Valley sits at roughly 2,900 to 3,000 feet in the Mojave Desert, where clear nights lose heat fast. The Town lists winters dipping into the low 20s and summers up to 110F, and that swing stresses pipe, insulation and fittings. A pipe that handles a hundred hot days can still split on one cold night.

Many homes here have the water heater in the garage, and many have irrigation lines above ground. Both are exposed. Insulate the first few feet of hot and cold lines at the heater and drain or protect irrigation lines. If your water comes from Liberty Utilities, which serves about 81 percent of the Town, your meter and the line to it are the utility's concern, while the house side is yours.

Pipe insulation makers publish installation guidelines, and following them on exposed lines in garages and attics gives better protection than loose wrapping. Seal the gaps first, then insulate.

If a pipe freezes anyway

If no water comes out of a tap in cold weather, a pipe may be frozen. Open the faucet and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or a heating pad, starting near the faucet and working back. Never use an open flame, a torch or a space heater placed too close.

If you cannot find the frozen section or you see a crack, close the main valve and call. Water pressure returns when the ice melts, and a split pipe can flood quickly. Photograph any damage and keep receipts for insurance. Leak detection can find a split in a wall.

Insurance, repairs and next steps

Sudden freeze damage is often covered by a homeowner policy, while gradual leaks are not. Photograph the damage, stop the water and call your agent. Dry the area within a day or two to avoid mold.

After a freeze, have the repair done properly and add insulation where the pipe failed. Homes in Victorville and Hesperia face similar nights, with a January low in Hesperia near 32F. We serve both from the same line.
