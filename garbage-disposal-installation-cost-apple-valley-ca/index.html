Garbage Disposal Installation in Apple Valley, CA: What Sets the Cost

Disposal prices swing with motor size, wiring and what is under the sink. Here are the factors that matter, what a quote should say and when a repair makes more sense than a new unit.

By the Apple Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 20264 min read

What goes into a disposal installation price

A garbage disposal installation looks simple from above, but the price is set by what sits under the sink. The unit itself, the motor size, the wiring and the drain connections all play a part. A straight swap of a similar unit takes less time than adding a disposal where there was none.

No fair quote can be given without knowing a few details. A plumber needs to know whether there is already a disposal, whether the sink has a dishwasher connection and whether there is an outlet and switch. With those answers, the work and price can be explained clearly before it starts.

Our garbage disposal repair page covers repairs and installs together, and this guide goes deeper on cost factors.

The factors that move the price

  • Motor size. Larger motors handle more and jam less, and cost more.
  • Feed type. Continuous feed is common, while batch feed adds a stopper and a different mechanism.
  • Sound insulation. Quieter units use more insulation and cost more.
  • Electrical work. An outlet or switch that must be added increases the scope.
  • Existing plumbing. A corroded trap or drain line may need replacement.
  • Dishwasher connection. A dishwasher drain hose connects to the unit and needs a knockout plug removed.

What an installation visit looks like

1
Check power and plumbing. We confirm there is a switch and outlet and look at the trap, drain and any dishwasher hose.
2
Remove the old unit. We disconnect the drain and unmount the old disposal from the sink flange.
3
Mount the new unit. We attach the mounting ring, seat the disposal and tighten the connections.
4
Reconnect and test. We reconnect the drain and dishwasher hose, restore power and run the unit with water to check for leaks.

Repair or replace

If a disposal is jammed or tripping its reset, a repair is often enough and costs far less than a new unit. If the housing is cracked or leaking, or the motor has burnt out, replacement is the sensible choice. A disposal that is more than a decade old and failing is also a candidate for replacement, since a repair buys limited time.

We tell you plainly which makes sense. Our approach is to explain what we see and let you choose, not to sell a new unit to everyone. If the unit is working but noisy, a different model may solve the noise.

Disposals, septic and hard water in Apple Valley

A disposal adds solids to a septic tank, which Town planning documents say serves about 70 percent of Apple Valley development. If your home is on septic, ask a septic professional how a disposal affects pumping, and use it lightly. Fats, coffee grounds and fibrous peels fill a tank faster than most people expect.

Liberty Utilities reported a 2022 hardness average near 80 ppm, so mineral scale builds in the trap and drain over time. Running cold water while the disposal runs and flushing for a few seconds afterward helps. A disposal in a hard-water home also benefits from a regular cleaning of the baffle and the trap.

Follow the maker's installation guidelines for the dishwasher knockout plug and the electrical hookup. If there is no outlet under the sink, have a licensed electrician add one.

Planning the project well

Before you buy a unit, check the space under the sink. A larger disposal needs room, and cabinets with a lot of storage may leave little. Measure the clearance and tell your plumber. If the sink has two bowls, the disposal normally goes in the main bowl.

If you plan a kitchen remodel, the order matters. Install the disposal after the sink and countertop are in, and before the cabinet is loaded again. Our plumbing installation page explains how we work alongside other trades.

Insurance, permits and warranties

A planned disposal installation is a home upgrade cost, and homeowner insurance does not normally pay for it. A sudden leak that damages a cabinet or floor may be treated differently. Permit rules for a disposal swap vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division if you are unsure.

Check the unit's warranty and keep the receipt. The warranty covers defects in the unit, and the installer's work is covered separately, so ask what each includes. For homes in Victorville and Hesperia, the same service applies.
