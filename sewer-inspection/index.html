Sewer camera inspection in Apple Valley, before you dig or buy

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible cable and runs it down the line from the cleanout. The picture shows the pipe material, the joints, roots, grease, scale, sags and breaks, and the distance to each problem. That information turns a guess into a plan.

Inspections help in three situations. After repeat backups, they find the reason the line keeps clogging. Before a repair, they locate the damage so the work is limited to the affected section. Before buying a house, they show the condition of a line the seller may not have looked at in decades.

Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow.

If sewage is backing up right now, use our emergency sewer line cleaning page. The camera usually follows the cleaning.

What a camera can show

  • Roots entering at joints and the extent of the growth.
  • Grease and scale coating the walls and narrowing the opening.
  • Belly or sag where the pipe has settled and holds water.
  • Cracks and breaks in clay, cast iron or plastic pipe.
  • Offset joints where sections have shifted apart.
  • Pipe material and connections, which affect repair options.

How the inspection works

1
Find the cleanout. We open the cleanout, or pull a toilet if there is no cleanout, to reach the line.
2
Run the camera. The camera moves along the pipe while we watch the live picture and note distances.
3
Locate problems. A locator on the surface can mark where an underground defect sits.
4
Review and advise. We show you the footage, explain what it means and outline repair options and prices.

Reading the results with you

Not everything on camera is a problem. Light scale, small surface roots and hairline marks may be fine, while a belly that holds a few inches of water, a joint that has dropped or a break with soil showing are real concerns. We explain which is which, and we say so when the pipe is in good shape.

A recording lets you share the footage with a second opinion, an insurer or a buyer. If a repair is needed, sewer line repair covers the options, from a spot repair to replacement.

A camera cannot see a septic tank or leach field. If your home is on septic, the camera covers the pipe from the house to the tank, and the tank itself needs a septic professional.

Why inspection matters in Apple Valley and the High Desert

Town planning documents describe about 30 percent of Apple Valley development as sewered and 70 percent as septic, so the first question is whether your home connects to the sewer. For a home on the sewer, the lateral line runs to the connection, and the Town owns the main. Wastewater is treated by the Victor Valley Wastewater Reclamation Authority in Victorville.

Older lines in the High Desert are often clay or cast iron, and trees and shrubs that seek water can send roots into joints. Gently sloping alluvial fans carry the soil, and settlement can leave a belly in a line. A camera shows what the soil has done to the pipe, and permit rules for any repair vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division.

According to the Contractors State License Board, you can look up a plumber's license online. It is worth doing before any repair that follows an inspection.

Source: Town of Apple Valley planning documents.

Buying a home with an older sewer line

A home inspection usually does not include a sewer camera. If the house is older or has mature trees near the line, ask for one as part of due diligence. The footage can support a repair request or a price discussion with the seller. We explain the findings in plain terms and do not push a repair that the pipe does not need.

What affects sewer inspection cost

  • Line length. A long run takes more time.
  • Access. A clear cleanout is faster than pulling a toilet.
  • Locating. Marking a defect on the surface is an added step.
  • Recording. Footage and a short summary add time.
  • Timing. Same-day and after-hours work is staffed differently from a scheduled visit.

Insurance note: a camera report can document the cause of a loss for a claim, and sewer line coverage varies by policy. Ask your agent and call (760) 986-1999 for your real number.
