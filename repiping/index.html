Repiping in Apple Valley when patching is no longer enough

Every plumbing system has a lifespan. Copper can develop pinholes from the inside, galvanized steel corrodes and chokes, and some older plastic pipe types fail early. When leaks keep appearing in different places or water pressure falls over the years, replacing the system or a large section is usually cheaper than paying for repeated repairs and the damage they cause.

A repipe replaces the supply pipes that bring water to fixtures, usually in copper or PEX. Partial repipes replace only a problem section, such as the line to a bathroom or the lines under a slab. A whole-home repipe replaces nearly all of them. Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow, and we explain the scope and price before work starts.

If you are not sure where the leak is, start with leak detection. If water is spraying now, burst pipe repair is the emergency page.

Signs the pipes are near the end of their life

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in copper or at the same joints.
  • Brown or rusty water when a tap first opens, a sign of corroding steel pipe.
  • Falling pressure across the home, not just at one fixture.
  • Visible green or white buildup on exposed copper.
  • Known problem pipe, such as galvanized steel or older polybutylene.
  • Leaks in walls or slab that repair only for a while.

How a repipe is done

1
Assess the home. We trace the lines, find the shutoff and check pressure, and decide whether a partial or whole-home repipe fits.
2
Plan the routes. New lines usually run through walls, ceilings and attic or crawlspace, with as few openings as the layout allows.
3
Replace the lines. We connect the new pipe to each fixture and to the main, with new shutoffs where needed.
4
Test and patch. We pressure test, restore water and leave access openings ready for drywall repair by you or another trade.

Copper or PEX

Copper is durable and familiar, though it can pit in some water and costs more. PEX is flexible plastic that goes in faster, handles freezing better and is resistant to mineral scale. Water chemistry, local practice and personal preference all play a role, and permit rules and code acceptance vary by town and job.

Our blog explains the choice in more depth for homeowners who want to compare, and we explain the pros and cons for your home when you call. We are straightforward about the trade-offs and do not push one material for every case.

The water service line from the meter to the house

The water service line runs from the meter at the street to the house. If it leaks, you may see a wet spot, hear running water with everything off or see a high water bill. Replacement involves trenching, and a call to Dig Alert at 811 is required at least two working days before digging in California.

The ownership split between utility and homeowner depends on the water purveyor, so ask yours. Liberty Utilities serves about 81 percent of Apple Valley, and the Town lists 11 water purveyors in total. We locate the leak, explain the repair or replacement and give you the price before we dig.

Apple Valley water and the plumbing in older homes

Liberty Utilities reports 2022 hardness from 57 to 130 ppm, averaging about 80 ppm, and Apple Valley sits in the Alto sub-unit of the Mojave groundwater basin. Water chemistry affects copper and fixtures over decades, and hard water leaves scale in older steel lines. If you want to know how your own water behaves, test your tap and read your purveyor's annual water quality report.

Winter lows in the low 20s mean exposed pipes in attics, crawlspaces and outside walls need insulation, and PEX handles freezing better than rigid pipe. Permit rules for repiping vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division.

Repiping follows the California Plumbing Code, the state standard for approved pipe materials and connections, and a permit and inspection are common. According to the Contractors State License Board, you can check a contractor's license online before you sign.

What affects repiping cost

  • Home size. More fixtures and longer runs take more pipe and time.
  • Material. Copper and PEX differ in price and labor.
  • Access. Slab foundations, two stories and finished ceilings make work harder.
  • Scope. Partial and whole-home projects are very different jobs.
  • Drywall and finish. Opening and patching walls is a separate trade from plumbing.
