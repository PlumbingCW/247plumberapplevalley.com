When Does a Water Heater Need Replacing in Apple Valley, CA?

Most water heaters give warning before they fail. Here are the signs that point to replacement, the signs that point to a simple repair and how local water and weather affect the decision.

By the Apple Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 29, 20264 min read

Repair or replace: the question behind every old heater

A water heater is a steel tank that corrodes from the inside over many years. When the tank itself fails, no repair helps. When a part fails, such as a thermostat, an element or a gas control, a repair can be good value. The skill is telling the two apart, and the age of the unit is the first clue.

Most tank heaters last roughly ten to fifteen years, depending on the water, the maintenance and the model. A unit in its second decade that has started to leak, rumble or run out of hot water is usually near the end. A young unit with a single failed part is usually worth fixing.

Our water heater repair page explains what we can fix, and this guide helps you decide when to stop repairing.

Signs a water heater is near the end of its life

  • Water around the base that is coming from the tank, not from a pipe or valve.
  • Rust-colored hot water, especially if cold water is clear.
  • Rumbling or popping from sediment hardened in the bottom of the tank.
  • Age over a decade, with a growing list of repairs.
  • Not enough hot water that a thermostat or element swap does not fix.
  • Visible corrosion on the tank, fittings or the relief valve.

How to check the age and condition

1
Find the serial number. The label on the tank has a serial number whose first characters often encode the month and year of manufacture. The maker's website explains the code.
2
Look for leaks and rust. Check around the base, the fittings and the drain valve with a flashlight and a dry paper towel.
3
Test the hot water. Run a hot tap and note whether the water is clear, hot enough and lasts as long as it used to.
4
Listen to the tank. Rumbling suggests sediment and a tank that is working hard.

When repair is the better call

If the unit is under ten years old and the problem is a heating element, thermostat, gas control valve or pilot, a repair often makes sense. Those parts fail on units that are otherwise sound, and replacing them costs far less than a new heater. A tank that is not leaking, with a failing part, is a good candidate.

Ask the plumber to explain why a repair will last. A fair plumber says what failed, what the part costs and what else might fail soon. If the tank is old, they will tell you a repair may only buy a short time, and let you choose.

Apple Valley water and weather shorten or extend heater life

Hard water speeds sediment buildup in a tank, and Liberty Utilities reported 2022 hardness from 57 to 130 ppm, averaging near 80 ppm. Moderately hard water is not harsh, but without a yearly flush a tank can fill with sediment that insulates the burner and speeds corrosion. A flush and an occasional anode rod check can add years.

Winter cold in Apple Valley reaches the low 20s, and a heater in an unheated garage works harder in January. Heat waves near 110F matter too, since a hot garage stresses the tank and the relief valve. A heater in a garage deserves insulated pipes and a drain pan with a line to a safe place.

According to the label and the manual, the serial number identifies when the unit was built, and the maker's website explains how to read it. Standard tank units are built with a limited service life.

Planning ahead beats an emergency

A heater that fails on its own schedule usually fails at a bad time, such as the coldest morning or a holiday weekend. If your unit is old, plan its replacement. You choose the model, compare quotes and schedule the work instead of paying for a rushed one.

If the heater is already leaking, shut off the water to it, turn off the power or gas and call. Our water heater replacement page is built for that case. For a planned swap, water heater installation explains the steps.

What to do before the heater fails

Know where the cold water shutoff, the gas valve and the breaker are. Put a drain pan under the heater with a line to a safe place. Flush the tank yearly and check the anode rod every few years. Write down the install date on the tank with a marker.

A leak sensor under the heater costs little and warns you early. For homes in Victorville and Hesperia, the same checklist applies, and we serve both.
