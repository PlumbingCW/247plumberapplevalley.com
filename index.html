Apple Valley sits at roughly 2,900 to 3,000 feet in the Mojave Desert, where winter nights can fall into the low 20s and summer afternoons climb toward 110 degrees. Both extremes stress pipes, hoses and water heaters, so failures often show up with the first hard freeze or the first heat wave.

Plumbing problems that should not wait until morning

Some jobs can wait for a scheduled visit. These cannot, because water and sewage damage floors, drywall and belongings by the hour.

  • A burst or split pipe. Water running from a wall, ceiling or slab spreads with every minute. Close the main valve and call.
  • Sewage backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain. Stop using water in the house and keep children and pets away from the area.
  • A leaking water heater. A drip can become a full tank release. Turn off power or gas to the heater and close the cold water inlet.
  • A toilet that overflows and will not stop. Close the supply valve behind the toilet, then call.
  • No water at all, or a sudden loss of pressure, especially after a hard freeze.
  • A smell of gas. Leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility before anyone else. Plumbing repairs wait until the area is declared safe.

While you wait: four steps that limit the damage

Whatever the problem, the first ten minutes decide how much of the house gets wet. These steps work for most plumbing emergencies and take no tools.

1
Find the main shut-off valve. It is usually where the water line enters the house, often near a front hose bib or the garage wall. Turn a round handle clockwise, or a lever a quarter turn, until it stops.
2
Open one faucet. Running a cold tap at the lowest point in the house drains what is left in the pipes and eases pressure on the break.
3
Mind the electricity. If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of that area. Cut power only if you can reach the switch from a dry spot.
4
Photograph and move belongings. Pictures help with an insurance claim, and lifting rugs, boxes and electronics off a wet floor saves more than most people expect.

What we can help with, from clogs to whole-house pipes

Most calls fall into a few families. Clogs and backups lead to drain cleaning, and stubborn or repeat blockages are often a job for hydro jetting once the line has been looked at. If sewage keeps coming back, the problem may sit deeper, which is where sewer line repair and a camera view through sewer inspection come in.

Hot water trouble splits into water heater repair when the unit can be saved and water heater replacement when it cannot. Planned upgrades such as a tankless water heater or a standard water heater installation are scheduled jobs with a price agreed in advance.

Leaks are their own category. A wet spot with no obvious source, a water meter that keeps moving or a bill that jumped all point toward leak detection, and a leak under the floor may turn out to be a slab leak. When a line has already failed, burst pipe repair stops the damage first.

Fixture jobs round out the list, including toilet repair, faucet repair and garbage disposal repair. Older homes with failing supply lines may need repiping, new fixtures and lines fall under plumbing installation, and businesses can use commercial plumbing for after-hours calls. If your water leaves spots and scale, ask about water softener and filtration.

Apple Valley plumbing: what the local water and sewer setup means for you

Most Apple Valley homes get water from Liberty Utilities, which serves about 81 percent of the Town through its Apple Valley Ranchos system. The water comes from deep wells in the Alto sub-unit of the Mojave groundwater basin. Liberty's 2022 data lists hardness between 57 and 130 ppm, with an average near 80 ppm, or about 4.7 grains per gallon.

That is moderate hardness, and scale can still collect in water heater tanks and on fixtures over the years, so a scheduled tank flush is worth the time.

Not every address is on Liberty. The Town lists 11 water purveyors, and Golden State Water is the second largest. Your water bill shows which utility serves you, and that name is the right one to call about a main-line leak or low pressure on the street side of your meter.

Wastewater is the bigger divide. The Town's planning documents describe roughly 30 percent of development as sewered and roughly 70 percent as septic. Tell us which one you have when you call, because a backup on the Town's sewer and a backup on a septic system point to different causes. Pumping a septic tank is separate work from plumbing repair, and we will say so if that is where the symptoms lead.

What affects the price of a plumbing job

We give a clear price before work starts. These are the factors that move it, so you know what is behind the number.

  • Cause and access. A clog in a sink trap and a break under a slab call for very different time, tools and labor.
  • Parts and fixture type. Common fixtures are easy to source, while a specialty heater, valve or cartridge can add time and parts cost.
  • Time of day and urgency. Work in the middle of the night is staffed differently from a scheduled visit, so ask how timing affects your quote.
  • Pipe age and condition. Corroded or galvanized lines can crumble when touched, which changes the repair plan.
  • Permits and code needs. Some jobs need a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division.
