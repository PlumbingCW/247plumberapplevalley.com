Water softener and filtration in Apple Valley, matched to your water

Hard water leaves white crust on faucets, spots on glassware and scale inside water heaters and pipes. Softeners remove hardness minerals using an ion exchange resin that is recharged with salt. Filters work differently, targeting things like chlorine taste, sediment or specific contaminants. The right system depends on what your water actually contains, so the first step is knowing your water.

We install softeners, whole-home sediment and carbon filters and drinking-water systems such as reverse osmosis. We do not sell a system before you know what problem it solves. Same-day appointments are available when schedules allow, and we state the price before work starts.

If scale is already hurting a heater, water heater repair can assess the damage.

Signs your water may need treatment

  • White crust on faucets and showerheads from mineral scale.
  • Spots on glassware and dishes after the dishwasher.
  • Dry skin and stiff laundry that many people link to hard water.
  • Low flow at fixtures from scale in aerators and valves.
  • Chlorine taste or smell in drinking water.
  • Sediment or cloudiness after plumbing work or a pipe break.

What Apple Valley water reports show, and what to do with them

Liberty Utilities, which serves about 81 percent of Apple Valley, reported a 2022 hardness range of 57 to 130 ppm with an average near 80 ppm. On the common USGS scale, 61 to 120 ppm is moderately hard and over 120 ppm is hard. So most Liberty customers sit in the moderate range, with some locations higher.

The Town lists 11 water purveyors, and each reports its own values. If you are not served by Liberty, find your water company's annual report. Neighboring Hesperia Water District reports a 2022 average near 75 ppm, and the City of Victorville Water District reported a 2025 average near 36 ppm. These numbers differ because sources and blending differ, so test your tap rather than assuming.

Hardness, septic and brine in the High Desert

Moderate hardness is a nuisance, not an emergency. A softener adds sodium or potassium to the water and discharges brine when it regenerates. About 70 percent of Apple Valley development is on septic according to Town planning documents, and brine discharge on a septic system is a question to ask a septic professional. Some homeowners choose a scale inhibitor or a point-of-use filter instead.

Where softeners connect also matters. The unit needs a drain, an electrical outlet and space, and the lines should keep outdoor spigots off the softened water. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Apple Valley Building and Safety Division.

Source: Liberty Utilities, Hesperia Water District and City of Victorville Water District water quality reports, and Town of Apple Valley planning documents.

Why test your tap before you buy

Water reports show what leaves the utility, while your tap shows what reaches you after pipes, a water heater and old fixtures. A cheap hardness test strip takes a minute, and a lab test costs more but covers more. Start there before spending on a system.

What affects softener and filter cost

  • System type. A softener, a carbon filter and reverse osmosis differ widely.
  • Capacity. A bigger household needs a larger unit.
  • Installation. Drains, outlets and bypass valves change the work.
  • Supplies. Salt, filters and membranes are ongoing costs.
  • Water test. A lab test costs more than a strip and gives more detail.
